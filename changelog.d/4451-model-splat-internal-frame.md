- **Android: a model at rest no longer costs anything on a rendered frame ([#4451](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4451)).** `ModelNode` and `SplatNode` were the two library node types still called on every frame, in full, because their class overrode `onFrame` — and a `ModelNode` parents every node of its glTF, so a scene made of models was walked node by node on every frame whatever it was doing. A model now tells its scene when it has something to do with a frame: while an animation is in `playingAnimations` (paused ones included), while it has a skin (its bone matrices are recomputed on every frame, so a skeleton posed by hand or through `animator` still reaches the mesh), and while one of its renderables has a bounding box still being watched (empty for now, or morph targets). A fully loaded model with no skin, no morph target and nothing playing is not visited at all, and neither are the nodes under it. `playAnimation` — or any entry added to `playingAnimations` — brings it back for the next frame, and a non-looping animation takes it out again on the frame it ends. A `SplatNode` is still asked on every frame whether the camera moved, since a camera reports its moves to nobody, but as one step instead of an opaque call. Unchanged: `node.onFrame = { }` on a model fires on every frame, before the model's own work as before; a class of your own that overrides `onFrame` on top of `ModelNode` or `SplatNode` is still called on every frame, in full. One thing to know: a map *assigned* to `playingAnimations` is kept as the node's storage and read back through the property — add entries through `modelNode.playingAnimations` or `playAnimation`, not through a reference kept to the map you assigned, or a model at rest will not notice until something else gives it a frame.
