- **`SceneView(contentPadding = …)`: the subject stays in the area a panel leaves free ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** Declare the part of the surface a bottom sheet, a side panel or a title bar covers and the visible rectangle becomes the camera's viewport: the optical centre moves to its centre and the lens spans it. The surface is not resized, the camera pose and the user's orbit are not touched, and the mapping is linear in the padding, so animating it with the panel's own curve makes the subject follow the panel. A tenth of the view always stays visible, whatever the padding. Also on `CameraNode.contentPadding`, in pixels (`ViewportPadding`): a projection you set yourself (`setProjection`, orthographic, custom) is kept and only scaled and shifted, and `autoFitContent` fits again when the padding changes. Not applied in AR, where the projection is the device camera's. The Android twin of `.contentInsets(_:)` on iOS.
- **`fitCameraToBounds` / `CameraNode.fitToBounds`: frame any box under a given angle ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** Where `fitDistanceForBounds` answers "how far" for a subject that may turn, the new fit frames a bounding box as it is seen from one direction and also returns where to aim, so the picture is centred and touches the requested fill on the axis that binds. The box needs no model behind it: a board, a room or a whole scene declares its bounds as an `Aabb`. With a content padding set, the fit uses the visible area's aspect ratio.

- **Picking under a shifted or scaled projection ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** `Camera.viewToRay`, `viewToWorld`, `worldToView` and `View.screenToRay` read Filament's projection getters, which leave `setScaling` and `setShift` out: a touch on a subject drawn off-centre was answered for where it would have been without the shift. They now go through the projection that is drawn (`Camera.renderedProjectionTransform`).
- **Demo app: demos that frame their scene between the title and the controls render in landscape ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** On a phone held sideways the two bands were taller than the window: the scene was laid out with no height, never drew a frame, and the loading cover ended on "stalled". When less than 160 dp would be left, the scene now stays full-frame under the controls.

- **Demo app: Rolling Balls runs edge to edge and keeps its board clear of the settings sheet ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** The stage now fills the screen under the title row and the controls, and the board is framed in the band they leave free. Open the settings sheet and the board slides up above it and stays whole — at its resting height and dragged all the way up — and a tap or a drag still lands the ball under the finger. In landscape the two rows of controls share one line and the board is centred over them, inside the window's safe insets, and the gesture hint steps aside whenever the free band is too short to hold both it and the board.
