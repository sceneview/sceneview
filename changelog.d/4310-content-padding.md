- **`SceneView(contentPadding = …)`: the subject stays in the area a panel leaves free ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** Declare the part of the surface a bottom sheet, a side panel or a title bar covers and the visible rectangle becomes the camera's viewport: the optical centre moves to its centre and the lens spans it. The surface is not resized, the camera pose and the user's orbit are not touched, and the mapping is linear in the padding, so animating it with the panel's own curve makes the subject follow the panel. Also on `CameraNode.contentPadding`, in pixels (`ViewportPadding`). Android only for now.
- **`fitCameraToBounds` / `CameraNode.fitToBounds`: frame any box under a given angle ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** Where `fitDistanceForBounds` answers "how far" for a subject that may turn, the new fit frames a bounding box as it is seen from one direction and also returns where to aim, so the picture is centred and touches the requested fill on the axis that binds. The box needs no model behind it: a board, a room or a whole scene declares its bounds as an `Aabb`. With a content padding set, the fit uses the visible area's aspect ratio.
<!-- category: Fixed -->
- **Picking under a shifted or scaled projection ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** `Camera.viewToRay`, `viewToWorld`, `worldToView` and `View.screenToRay` read Filament's projection getters, which leave `setScaling` and `setShift` out: a touch on a subject drawn off-centre was answered for where it would have been without the shift. They now go through the projection that is drawn (`Camera.renderedProjectionTransform`).
<!-- category: Changed -->
- **Demo app: Rolling Balls re-centres its board above the settings sheet ([#4310](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4310)).** Where the settings sheet reaches the board — a smaller display size, a taller sheet — the board slides up into the area left above it and stays whole, and a tap still drops the ball under the finger. At the default display size the sheet stops short of the board and nothing moves.
