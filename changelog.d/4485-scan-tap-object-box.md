- **Demo app: tap a piece of furniture in a scan's replay to read its size ([#4485](https://github.com/sceneview/sceneview/issues/4485)).** In the replay of a Room Scan, a single tap on an object lights the points it is made of, draws a box round it and writes its length, depth and height in metres next to it. A tap on another object picks that one; a tap on the floor, on a wall or in the void clears it. The room stops turning by itself as soon as a finger touches it, and does not start again while a box is shown — a double-tap still reframes it; orbit, pan and pinch are unchanged and the box follows them. The box is worked out on the phone from the points the scan already stored — no model, no download: the floor and the walls are set aside, the object is grown from the tapped point, and the box is the tightest rectangle round its footprint. A wall the scan saw too little of to recognise is cut away from the furniture standing against it, and gives no box by itself. Anything longer than two thirds of the room (never more than three metres) gets "Too big for one box" instead of a wrong size; a shard of depth noise — under 30 × 20 cm, or less than 0.2 m² of seen surface — gets no box and does not hide the furniture behind it. A tap a fingertip beside a thin object (a lamp's pole) finds it, and a plant, too sparse to grow through, is boxed with a looser net. A sliver under 30 cm deep that the scan cut off a larger piece of furniture (a daybed seen in two passes a few centimetres apart) is joined back to it before the box is drawn, while a thin object standing alone — a console, a wall shelf — keeps its own box, and so does a small object next to larger furniture: the join never makes a box longer than 1.5 m, or than the sliver itself by more than 30 cm. The box stays selected through a visit to the Camera view and back, and when it would be drawn under the replay's Camera card or the map's stats card the view slides just enough to show it, with its dimensions placed beside those cards, never under them. The label appears where it will stay — it is placed for the end of that slide, not moved there afterwards — and a box that an orbit, a pan or a pinch has turned under the cards or the dock is slid back into view once the finger has let go and the spin has died. On a small phone, where the cards and the dock leave only a narrow band of the view, the box is brought whole into the room beside the Camera card — the view steps back and the box is drawn smaller rather than left with a corner behind a card, the "Build 3D model" pill or the dock. Known limits: the sizes are those of the part the scan saw, so a sofa scanned from one side reads short; two pieces of furniture standing against each other can come out as one box; a piece of furniture longer than two thirds of its room is refused rather than measured; the camera view of the replay has no picking — its 3D thumbnail opens the 3D view, where it works.
