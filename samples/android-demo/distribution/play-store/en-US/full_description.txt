See what the SceneView SDK can do, on your own phone, before you write a line of code.

SceneView is an open-source 3D and augmented reality SDK for Jetpack Compose, SwiftUI and the web, built on ARCore on Android. This app is its demo: more than 40 screens, each one a sample of a feature you can put in your own app. The source of every screen is on GitHub, and the SDK call at its heart is often only a few lines long.

START HERE

- Ask in AR (beta) — ask out loud where to hang a picture, whether a 2 m sofa fits or how to bleed a radiator, and the answer is drawn in your room, on the real walls and floor. Answers are prepared ones, picked on your phone from what you ask; measurements are approximate, and questions are in English for now.
- The home — a featured model in a live 3D scene. Open it to turn it around on screen, or place it in your room at its real size.
- Matter — press a slab of memory foam, gel, velvet, leather, marble or brushed steel. Foam sinks under your finger and comes back slowly, steel does not move, and your phone's vibration changes with each material.
- Cosmos — a galaxy, a particle collision, a flow field and a ringed star system, joined by a camera tour that flies you through them. Touch the screen to take the camera back.
- 3D Scan — walk around a room and watch its walls appear in 3D as you go, with the area covered counting up. On phones with ARCore depth, it then draws an indicative floor plan of the room's real shape, with its doors, windows, dimensions and area, which you can share as a PDF or a DXF drawing. Missed a corner? Scan that part again and it joins the room. No room at hand? Tap "See an example" to replay a recorded scan in 3D, as a map, or through the camera.

WHAT THE SAMPLES SHOW

- 3D models — load GLB and glTF files, play their animations, and orbit, pinch and zoom the camera. Tap "Surprise me" for the next model.
- Augmented reality — place a toy car, a sofa or a coffee mug on the floor or a table, hang something flat against a wall, track images and faces, and measure distances, walls, doors and windows.
- Real-world blending — real furniture and people stay in front of virtual objects, and the room's own light lights the model.
- Lighting and materials — swap HDR environments and watch reflections and shadows change in real time.
- Anchors, depth, physics, geometry, spatial audio and more.
- Your own files — open a GLB, glTF, OBJ, STL or PLY file and see how the SDK renders it.

Every screen follows your phone's light or dark theme.

FOR DEVELOPERS

A 3D model on screen is one composable, and AR placement is one call. Find the screen that looks like what you want to build, find its code on GitHub, and copy it into your project. The SDK, the samples and this app are open source: SceneView has more than 1,300 stars on GitHub, and over 500 public repositories declare it as a dependency.

PRIVACY

No account, no ads, no cross-app tracking. Models are rendered on your device. A scan you record stays on your device until you delete it; sharing sends a copy you choose.

The app sends usage statistics and crash reports through Firebase, which uses an app-installation identifier, to decide what to improve next. Turn both off in About › Privacy & notifications.

Some features use the network: browsing and downloading online models, the HD model pack (downloaded on Wi-Fi), downloads of on-device AI models, a streamed demo video, optional notifications and app updates. The cloud AR samples (Cloud Anchors, Geospatial, Streetscape) send visual data, and for Geospatial your location, to Google's ARCore service. Ask in AR listens through your phone's speech recognition, which may send your voice to its provider, and the object-labelling sample uses Google's ML Kit, which may send its own diagnostics to Google.
