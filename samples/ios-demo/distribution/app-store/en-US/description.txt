See what the SceneView SDK can do, on your own iPhone or iPad, before you write a line of code.

SceneView is an open-source 3D and augmented reality SDK for SwiftUI, built on RealityKit. This app is its demo: nearly 40 screens, each one a sample of a feature you can put in your own app. The source of every screen is on GitHub, and the SDK call at its heart is often only a few lines long.

START HERE

- Cosmos — a galaxy, a star with a ringed planet, and particle storms that glow in real time. Tap the planet and the camera flies there.
- 3D Scan — move around a space and watch it take shape in 3D.

WHAT THE SAMPLES SHOW

- 3D models — load USDZ and Reality files, play their animations, and orbit, pinch and zoom the camera.
- Augmented reality — place a model on a table, hang it on a wall, track images and faces, or place it instantly without waiting for a plane.
- Real-world blending — real furniture and passing people stay in front of virtual objects, and the light around you lights the model.
- Lighting and materials — swap HDR environments and watch reflections and shadows change in real time.
- Scene mesh, depth, physics, geometry, spatial audio and more.
- Your own files — open a USDZ, Reality, STL, OBJ or PLY file and see how the SDK renders it.

FOR DEVELOPERS

A 3D model on screen is one view, and AR placement is one call. Find the screen that looks like what you want to build, find its code on GitHub, and copy it into your project. The SDK, the samples and this app are open source: SceneView has more than 1,300 stars on GitHub, and over 500 public repositories declare it as a dependency.

PRIVACY

No account, no ads, no cross-app tracking. Models are rendered on your device, and the camera is used only for AR: nothing it sees is uploaded. A scan you record stays on your device until you delete it; sharing sends a copy you choose.

The app sends usage statistics and crash reports through Firebase, which uses an app-installation identifier, to decide what to improve next. Turn both off in About › Privacy & notifications.

Some features use the network: browsing and downloading online models, the high-definition models (one is downloaded on Wi-Fi at first launch, the others when a sample needs them), a check for app updates on the App Store, and optional notifications.
